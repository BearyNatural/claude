# Plant & location data — review worklist

This file lists what a horticulturist should check before a commercial release. Everything here is visible in the app: each plant page shows its sources, its review status and "Not recorded" where data is missing.

## Status key

- **source-checked**: planting windows, spacing, germination and maturity trace back to cited sources. Other fields may still be general knowledge (see the last column).
- **draft**: key facts are still missing or unsourced.
- **general knowledge**: widely repeated gardening knowledge entered without a specific citation (source id `general-knowledge`).
- **BearyNatural heuristic** (`bn-heuristic`): every household quantity, succession interval, task duration and weather threshold. These are planning estimates by design and need expert tuning, not citations.

## Source anomalies and interpretations to review

1. **Gardening Australia monthly lists are occasionally inconsistent from month to month.** They were used as published, with these exceptions:
   - *Garlic, cool zone:* the monthly lists show Jul–Aug, but GA's own garlic guide says to plant in autumn in temperate and cool areas. The app uses **Mar–May** (source `ga-garlic`).
   - *Sweet potato:* GA lists it only in February for temperate areas and not at all for cool areas. The app uses SGA's "after the last frost" advice, **interpreted** as Oct–Dec (warm temperate) and Nov–Dec (cool temperate).
   - *Coriander, cool zone* and *spinach, tropical zone* aren't listed by GA. The app uses The Seed Collection chart months.
2. **Arid zone:** GA lists tomato, capsicum and chilli as plantable all year. The app's winter-frost rule (frost-tender crops in Jun–Aug where frost exposure is moderate or heavy → "better to wait") softens this. It still needs expert review for Alice Springs, Mildura and Dubbo-type climates.
3. **Tropical garlic in November** and **subtropical coriander in Nov–Dec** appear in GA's lists. The app's bolting and heat checks will usually advise waiting in hot weather. Check whether these months should be removed.
4. **The Seed Collection chart column order** is sowing depth, *row* spacing, then *plant* spacing, confirmed from the chart's legend (≡ row, ↔ plant).
5. **Timing basis:** the chart doesn't say whether "days to maturity" counts from sowing or from transplanting. The app follows the seed-industry convention: transplanted crops (tomato, cherry tomato, capsicum, chilli, eggplant, broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, leek) count from transplanting, and everything else counts from sowing (`timing.basis`). The flowering figures for those transplanted crops are general knowledge.
6. **Germination soil temperatures** come from OSU Extension, converted from °F and rounded. They're physiological thresholds, not region-specific.
7. **Blueberry:** "don't add lime, dolomite or mushroom compost" is general knowledge (the reason is given), not a citation from ABGA.
8. **Strawberry:** SGA lists "all year" planting for warm, temperate and cool areas. The tropical window (Mar–Jun) is general knowledge.
9. **Fig and mango** have no sourced planting months, so they're shown as *No local timing data yet*.
10. **Rosemary** has no sourced window, because The Seed Collection lists it without sowing months.
11. **Bolting thresholds** (lettuce 29 °C, spinach 26 °C, coriander 27 °C, rocket and pak choy 28 °C, radish 30 °C) are heuristics.
12. **Weather thresholds** are heuristics: frost risk when the forecast minimum is ≤ 2 °C (air at 2 m), extreme heat ≥ 35 °C, heavy rain ≥ 25 mm per day, and meaningful rain ≥ 5 mm.

## Companion planting

Every relationship in `src/data/companions.ts` has reasons and an evidence level. The *plausible* and *traditional* ones rely on general reasoning or tradition. The two *established* ones are potato–tomato (shared Solanaceae diseases) and strawberry after nightshades (SGA). A reviewer should confirm the evidence levels and add citations.

## Localities

- There are **156** reference towns. **27** of them are named as zone examples by the cited references (ABC Organic Gardener, The Seed Collection) and are marked `zoneFromReference`. The rest were assigned by the developer from general climate knowledge.
- The frost exposure for each town is a coarse typical value. Towns worth a specific check: Toowoomba, Warwick, Stanthorpe, Dalby, Kingaroy and Gatton (Darling Downs and Lockyer frost); Tamworth and other inland NSW towns; Albury, Wagga Wagga and Dubbo (classed arid by the cited references); Geraldton, Carnarvon, Albany and Manjimup (WA); Emerald and Charters Towers (inland QLD).
- Coordinates are approximate town centres (2 decimal places).
